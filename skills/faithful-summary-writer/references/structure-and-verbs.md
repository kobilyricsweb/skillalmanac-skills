## Point-first template

1. The conclusion or outcome, with its single most important figure.
2. Two to four supporting figures, each with unit, period and comparison base.
3. The caveat or cost the source states (staffing, uncertainty, missing measurement).
4. Who decides what next, and when, if the source says.

Use for reports, news items, memos and results. Executive level keeps only items 1 and 3.

## Chronological template

1. One sentence of context: who, what, when it started.
2. The events in source order, each with its date or time marker.
3. The outcome or current state, last.
4. Unresolved items the source names.

Use for calls, interviews, incident write-ups and minutes. Keep the order of the source even if a later event matters more; say so in the outcome sentence instead.

## Argument-evidence template

1. The author's thesis, attributed ("The authors argue").
2. The strongest evidence offered, with its figure.
3. The main objection or limitation the author admits, or the strongest one a named critic raises.
4. What the author concludes the reader should do, if anything.

Use for papers, opinion pieces, proposals and legal arguments. Do not add your own objections.

## Reporting verbs and what they commit you to

| Source verb | Safe summary verb | Do not write |
|---|---|---|
| suggests, indicates weakly | suggests | shows, proves, confirms |
| found, measured | found, measured | established, proved |
| claims, asserts, alleges | claims, says | states as fact, reveals |
| plans, intends, proposes | plans, proposes | will, has decided |
| recommended | recommended | required, decided |
| estimated at | is estimated at, about | is, totals |
| said | said | admitted, argued (adds stance) |

## Hedge words to carry over

may, might, could, possibly, perhaps, probably, likely, unlikely, appears, seems, suggests, reportedly, allegedly, approximately, roughly, estimated, potentially, tends to. A hedge carries over to the sentence that holds the claim, even if you merge two source sentences.

## Common unfaithful moves

- Merging two sentences so a hedge applies to the wrong half, or a cause appears where the source only listed two facts.
- Comparing figures the source did not compare, or dropping the base ("up 18%" instead of "up 18% against the same weeks last year").
- Attributing an author's view to the institution, or the reverse.
- Turning a list of reasons into "mainly because" when the source gave no ranking.
- Tidying away a contradiction the source leaves open.
- Adding a closing evaluation sentence ("a promising result").
