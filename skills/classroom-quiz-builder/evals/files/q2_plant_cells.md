# Lesson notes: How leaves make food

*Grade 6 science, Unit 4. Class handout.*

## The big idea

Plants do not eat food the way animals do. They make their own, in a process called photosynthesis. The word means "putting together with light". A plant takes in two simple ingredients, water and carbon dioxide, and uses the energy of light to turn them into glucose, a kind of sugar. Oxygen is released as a leftover product.

## Where the ingredients come from

Water enters through the roots and travels up the stem in narrow tubes called xylem. Carbon dioxide comes from the air. It enters the leaf through tiny openings called stomata, most of which are on the underside of the leaf. Each stoma is bordered by two guard cells. When the plant has plenty of water, the guard cells swell and the opening widens. When the plant is short of water, the guard cells go limp and the opening closes. This saves water, but it also means less carbon dioxide gets in, so photosynthesis slows down on a hot, dry afternoon.

## Where it happens

Photosynthesis happens inside chloroplasts, small green structures found mainly in the cells near the top surface of the leaf. Chloroplasts are green because they contain chlorophyll, a pigment that absorbs red and blue light and reflects green light. That reflected green light is what we see. Roots have no chloroplasts, which is why roots are not green and cannot make food.

## What happens to the sugar

The plant uses some glucose straight away for energy. Some is joined into long chains called starch and stored, often in roots or seeds. A potato is mostly stored starch. Some is turned into cellulose, the tough material that makes up cell walls. Sugar that needs to move to other parts of the plant travels in tubes called phloem, which run alongside the xylem.

## A class experiment

In our experiment we covered part of a geranium leaf with black paper for two days, then tested the leaf with iodine. Iodine turns blue-black where starch is present. The uncovered part of the leaf turned blue-black. The covered part stayed the yellow-brown colour of the iodine. This shows that the leaf made starch only where light reached it.

## Remember

- Ingredients: water and carbon dioxide.
- Energy source: light.
- Products: glucose and oxygen.
- Place: chloroplasts.
- At night there is no light, so photosynthesis stops, but the plant keeps using stored sugar for energy.
