# The Rise and Fall of Port Calder's Herring Trade

## Q1 [recall]
After the canal to Marrow opened, how long did a barrel of salted herring take to reach Marrow by barge?
A. One day
B. Two days
C. Nine days
D. Eleven days
Answer: B
Source: "After it, the trip took two days by barge."
Why: B is right; nine days was the trip by cart before the canal, which is the most tempting wrong option.

## Q2 [understand]
Why was the salt store the first public building in Port Calder?
A. Because the town council needed a place to hold meetings
B. Because the families needed a dry place to keep their boats
C. Because visitors wanted somewhere to buy smoked fish
D. Because the catch could not travel to distant cities unsalted
Answer: D
Source: "without salt the catch could not be preserved for the journey to distant cities"
Why: D is right; the reading says the town had no town hall for its first forty years, so A is wrong.

## Q3 [understand]
Between 1820 and 1850 the fleet grew from 40 boats to 210 boats. What does the reading give as the reason?
A. Cheaper and faster transport let fishermen sell far more fish
B. Steam-powered boats let captains fish in bad weather
C. Coopers became so well paid that more people took up fishing
D. The town council paid for new boats to help the trade grow
Answer: A
Source: "Cheaper and faster transport meant that fishermen could sell far more fish"
Why: A is right; steam boats did not arrive until 1874, so B belongs to a later period.

## Q4 [recall]
What did the regional government do in 1988?
A. Built a new canal to the city of Marrow
B. Opened the old salt store as a museum
C. Declared Calder Bay a protected breeding area
D. Brought back the post of harbor master
Answer: C
Source: "In 1988 the regional government declared Calder Bay a protected breeding area"
Why: C is right; the museum opened in 1956, which makes B the most tempting wrong option.

## Q5 [understand]
What was the purpose of the Net Rule of 1838?
A. To let young herring escape and grow into adult fish
B. To keep the price of herring from falling in Marrow
C. To stop boats from fishing during the autumn season
D. To make sure every boat caught the same amount
Answer: A
Source: "so that young herring could escape and grow"
Why: A is right; the rule banned small-mesh nets and said nothing about prices or seasons.

## Q6 [recall]
According to the reading, why did most captains obey the Net Rule?
A. Large-mesh nets were cheaper for them to buy
B. The council fined each captain a barrel of herring
C. The coopers would not fill barrels with small fish
D. The harbor master checked nets and could hold a boat in port
Answer: D
Source: "Most captains obeyed it because the harbor master inspected nets on the dock and could hold a boat in port for a week."
Why: D is right; the reading names the harbor master's inspections and the week-long hold as the reason, not fines.

## Q7 [understand]
Why did small-mesh nets return to Port Calder after 1861?
A. The council repealed the Net Rule to help the fleet grow
B. The council ended the harbor master's job, so no one enforced the rule
C. The council cut the mesh limit from one inch to half an inch
D. The council allowed small nets only for steam boats
Answer: B
Source: "the council abolished the post of harbor master to save money. The Net Rule stayed on the books, but nobody enforced it"
Why: B is right; the rule stayed on the books, which rules out A, C and D.

## Q8 [recall]
In which year did Port Calder have its record catch?
A. 1861
B. 1874
C. 1879
D. 1890
Answer: C
Source: "The record catch came in 1879."
Why: C is right; 1874 is when steam boats arrived and 1890 is when the catch had fallen to under a quarter of the record.

## Q9 [apply]
A lake town bans tiny-mesh fishing nets, then cuts the money it pays lake inspectors. Using what happened in Port Calder, what is most likely?
A. People will follow the rule just as closely as before
B. The rule will prove to be a bad idea and should be dropped
C. The lake will stay safe because the rule is still written down
D. Many fishers will start using small nets again over time
Answer: D
Source: "The Net Rule failed not because it was a bad idea, but because the town stopped paying someone to enforce it."
Why: D is right; Port Calder's rule stayed on the books but small nets returned once no one enforced it, so C is wrong.

## Q10 [apply]
A fishing town sees its catches rise for several years, but the fish caught get younger and smaller each year. What should the town conclude, based on Port Calder's story?
A. The rising catches may hide a falling number of fish that survive to breed
B. The fishery is healthy because the total catch is still going up
C. The town should expect catches to keep rising for decades
D. Smaller fish mean more boats are needed to fix the problem
Answer: A
Source: "Catches rose at first, which made the town feel richer than ever. The record catch came in 1879. But the fish being caught were younger and smaller each year, and fewer survived to breed."
Why: A is right; Port Calder's catch fell below a quarter of its record by 1890, so B and C are wrong.
