The field survey of the harbour towns was made in the spring of 1987 by a small team. Every morning the team collected samples from the quay, and every evening they compared notes on the modern methods they had read about. A modern harbour has a long quay, and the old quay at Marrow Point was no exception to that rule. The team measured 1,250 metres of wall and logged 42 boats.

In the third week the weather turned. The self-control of the crew was tested, and the self-control of the captain was not. No boat left the quay for days, and a modern chart of the coast was no help in time. Each small boat stayed in port and each boat that sailed came back with few samples. By the tirne the storm passed, the report dated l987 was a month late.
