# Campaign brief: Ledgerly recurring invoices launch

Status: DRAFT for team review. Built from Tuesday's kickoff notes. Several inputs are missing or conflict; they are listed under Assumptions and open questions and must be settled before spend starts.

## Objective
Primary objective: new trial signups from a baseline of 430 in the six-week window to a target of 711 by 17 Mar 2027, six weeks after the 3 Feb 2027 launch. [calc: baseline 310 a month x 6 weeks / 4.33 weeks a month = 430; target = baseline + 281 campaign trials from the funnel below]
Secondary: (1) Paid subscribers from campaign trials, about 62, read on 16 Apr 2027 after each trial's 30 days. [calc: 281 trials x 22% trial-to-paid [given]]. (2) Landing-page trial conversion of at least 5.2% on search traffic [given: last quarter's average].
Why now: The recurring invoices feature ships on 3 Feb 2027 [given] and Billfold launched the same feature in October [given], so launch week is when people looking for the feature can find us.

## Audience
Freelancers who bill the same clients every month and want those invoices sent without retyping them, and who are already searching for invoicing or recurring billing, or are already on our email list. Reached through Google search and email. [assumption: this segment definition is ours and needs the team's agreement]
Not targeting: agencies and teams with staff who send invoices, and people already paying for Ledgerly. [assumption: exclusions are a proposal]

## Offer and message
Offer: 30-day free trial, then the Solo plan at $12 a month [given]. No launch discount or deadline was named [assumption: none offered].
Message: Set up a recurring invoice once and Ledgerly sends it every month.
Proof: The feature ships on 3 Feb 2027 [given] and sends invoices each month, as described in the notes [given]. No customer data supports any outcome claim. Claims needing substantiation: "get paid 2x faster" is NOT to be used (legal: no data, Noa). Any comparison with Billfold needs substantiation before use.
Call to action: Start your 30-day free trial.

## Budget
Total budget: $18,000 [given]
Split: 90% to search and 10% reserve [assumption]. This split is ours, not the team's. The total covers media only. It excludes creative and landing-page production (in-house, Dan and Priya), the email tool fee, and staff time. No cost was given for any of these [assumption: excluded].
Not used: Sam's remark on the call of "20 if we really need it" was never confirmed, so the plan uses $18,000 [given] only (see open questions).
Cost per paying subscriber: at the target, about $261 of media per paying subscriber [calc], which is $16,200 / 62 [calc]; that is about 22 months of list-price revenue at $12 a month [given], since $261 / $12 = 21.75 [calc]. The campaign does not pay back inside six weeks. It is a bet that trials convert and stay.

Path from money to the objective:
- Search: $16,200 [assumption] / $3.10 cost per click [given], last quarter's average, which may not hold for feature keywords = 5,226 clicks [calc: 16,200 / 3.10 = 5,225.8]
- 5,226 clicks x 5.2% landing-to-trial [given], last quarter's rate = 272 trials [calc: 271.7]
- Email: 8,400 subscribers [given] x 2% click rate [assumption] = 168 clicks [calc: 8,400 x 0.02]
- 168 clicks x 5.2% [assumption], applying the search rate to email traffic = 9 trials [calc: 8.7]
- Campaign trials: 272 + 9 = 281 [calc]
- Baseline: 310 trials a month [given] x 6 weeks / 4.33 weeks a month = 430 [calc: 429.6]
- Target: 430 + 281 = 711 [calc]. This assumes campaign trials are additional to baseline. If some baseline signups would have come anyway from the same searches, the real increase is smaller [assumption].
- Paid: 281 trials x 22% trial-to-paid [given; assumed to apply to campaign trials, assumption] = 62 paying subscribers [calc: 61.8]; 62 x $12 = $744 a month in recurring revenue [calc]
- Cost per trial: $16,200 / 281 = $58 [calc]. Cost per paying subscriber: $16,200 / 62 = $261 [calc].
- Check: 281 trials x 22% [given] = 62 paying subscribers [calc]. The paying figure is an estimate; the 22% rate is from the current base, not from campaign users [assumption].

The target of 711 is derived from the funnel, not from Sam's goal. Sam asked for "a big number" and none was given. If the team wants a larger number, the levers are more budget, a longer window, or better rates, and each needs evidence [assumption].

## Risks and guardrails
1. Search costs run above $3.10 per click for feature keywords, and Billfold's October launch raises competition. Early sign: week-one cost per click above $3.10 [given]. Response: apply the cost-per-trial rule and cut the weakest ad groups before buying more.
2. Only people already searching are reached, and that pool may be small. Early sign: search impressions and clicks well below the 5,226 planned clicks at the week-two review [calc]. Response: report the shortfall at the first review and decide whether to fund a demand-creation test. No TikTok or creator spend without an owner decision.
3. The email list is mostly existing free users and old trials, who may not convert. Early sign: click rate under 1% [assumption] on the first send. Response: review the offer before a second send.
4. Claims: "get paid 2x faster" is prohibited until we measure it and legal clears it. No comparison with Billfold without evidence. Every product claim in ads and email is checked by Noa before publication.
5. Payback: at 62 paying subscribers and $261 of media per subscriber [calc], the campaign loses money unless trial-to-paid or retention is well above the 22% trial-to-paid rate [given]. Early sign: the 16 Apr read-out below 62 paying subscribers [calc]. Response: review channel spend before any second campaign.
