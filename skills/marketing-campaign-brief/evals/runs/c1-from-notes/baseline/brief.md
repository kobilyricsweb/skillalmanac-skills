# Campaign brief: Ledgerly recurring invoices launch

Status: draft for team review. Items marked **OPEN** need a decision before the plan is final.

## 1. Summary

Ledgerly launches recurring invoices on **Feb 3, 2027**. The feature lets a freelancer set up an invoice once and have it send itself each month. The campaign runs for **6 weeks from launch** (about Feb 3 to Mar 16, 2027) and aims to drive trial signups for the $12/month Solo plan (30-day free trial).

The goal is to get signups. Sam wants a "big number" but has not given one (see OPEN items).

## 2. Product and offer

- **Product:** Ledgerly, invoicing app for freelancers.
- **Feature:** recurring invoices. Set once, sends itself monthly.
- **Plan:** Solo, $12/month, 30-day free trial.
- **Launch date:** Feb 3, 2027.

## 3. Goal

- **Primary:** new trial signups during the 6-week window.
- **Paid conversions:** not set. Trial-to-paid was 22% over the last 3 months, so paid conversions are a downstream number to track.
- **OPEN - headline target:** Sam wants a "big number" but has not given one. The team should agree a number before launch. For scale, see section 6.

## 4. Baseline numbers (from Priya)

| Metric | Value | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Trial signups per month | 310 | Average of last 3 months |
| Trial to paid | 22% | Same period |
| Email list | 8,400 subscribers | Mostly existing free users and old trials |
| Google search ads, avg CPC | $3.10 | Last quarter |
| Landing page to trial, search traffic | 5.2% | Last quarter |
| Google search spend | $6,000 | Last quarter total |

Derived from the above (simple arithmetic, not measured by the team):

- Cost per trial from search: $3.10 / 5.2% = about **$59.60**.
- Cost per paid conversion from search: $59.60 / 22% = about **$271**. This assumes trial-to-paid is the same for search-sourced trials, which is not verified.
- Baseline trials over the 6-week window with no campaign: about 310 x 1.4 months = **about 430**.

Gaps: the notes do not say how many clicks or trials the $6,000 produced, nor the churn or lifetime value of a paid subscriber. Both are needed to judge return on spend.

## 5. Budget

- **Budget: $18,000.**
- **OPEN - budget may be $20,000.** Later in the call Sam said "20 if we really need it". Nobody confirmed it. Plan on $18,000 until it is approved.

Suggested split for discussion (not approved):

- Google search ads: the one channel with measured numbers. Most of the budget goes here first.
- Email to the 8,400 list: no media cost, only Priya's and Dan's time.
- Social (TikTok or other): see section 7. Only a small, capped test, and only if approved.

Team time: Priya full time on marketing. Dan (design) 2 days per week. No agency and no creator budget.

## 6. What the budget can buy (search only, rough)

At $59.60 per trial (section 4), a search-only plan gives roughly:

- $18,000: about **300 trials** from search alone.
- $20,000: about **335 trials** from search alone.

This assumes the same CPC and conversion rate as last quarter. A launch campaign may see different CPCs (usually higher as competition rises, and a competitor is already in the market) and different conversion rates. Treat these as a starting point, not a forecast.

A realistic headline target should be set against about 430 baseline trials plus the campaign's own trials. Proposed approach for the team to confirm: set the target once the search test has one week of real data.

## 7. Channels

### Google search ads (primary, measured)
- Known CPC and landing page conversion.
- Start here, watch cost per trial weekly, and shift budget to what converts.

### Email (no media cost)
- 8,400 subscribers, mostly free users and old trials. Expect a different response from paying-prospect audiences.
- Use it for launch announcement and a follow-up sequence. Segment by free users versus old trials if the email tool allows it.

### Social / TikTok (OPEN)
- Sam wants to be on TikTok. The team has **never run TikTok or any creator work**, and there is **no benchmark**. Any spend would be a test with unknown results.
- Recommended handling for the team to decide: either (a) skip for this campaign, or (b) run a small capped test with a fixed budget and a clear stop rule, agreed before launch. Do not commit the main budget to it.
- No creator or influencer spend in this plan.

## 8. Messaging

Approved claims and limits:

- **Do not say "get paid 2x faster".** Legal (Noa) says there is no data for it. This may be reconsidered after launch if the team measures it.
- Stick to what the feature does: set up an invoice once, and it sends itself each month. Describe it in plain terms.
- Mention the 30-day free trial and the $12/month Solo price where accurate.

Competitive context:

- **Billfold** launched the same feature in October. The campaign should not assume it is first to market. Differentiation (for example, price, ease of setup, or other features) is not defined in the notes, so it is **OPEN**.

Audience:

- Freelancers who invoice clients. The notes do not define segments further. Confirm targeting for search keywords and email segments.

## 9. Timeline (6 weeks from launch)

- **Before launch (before Feb 3, 2027):** agree goal number, budget ($18k vs $20k), social decision, and differentiation. Set up tracking for search and email. Dan produces creative for 2 days per week.
- **Week 1 (Feb 3 onward):** launch announcement to email list. Search campaigns live. Review cost per trial after 7 days.
- **Weeks 2-5:** optimise search spend toward what converts. Run follow-up emails. Review weekly.
- **Week 6 (ends about Mar 16, 2027):** final results. Compare against goal and baseline (about 430 trials). Note whether a 2x-faster claim can be measured for a future campaign.

## 10. Measurement

- Trials per channel per week (search, email, any social).
- Cost per trial per channel.
- Landing page to trial rate (baseline 5.2% for search).
- Trial to paid rate (baseline 22%), tracked for campaign trials once they have had time to convert. Note that a 30-day trial means paid results for late-campaign trials arrive after the window ends.
- Churn and lifetime value: not in the notes. Needed to judge return on spend.
