# Campaign brief: Spring bakery promo (first draft)

## Objective
Primary objective: spring-promo orders (promo code redeemed) from 0 [assumption] to 60 [assumption] by 2027-05-31 [assumption]. No baseline was supplied, so the first two weeks confirm the starting point.
Secondary: new customers among the promo orders; sign-ups to the bakery's customer list.
Why now: spring is a natural window for a seasonal offer, and the owner needs to confirm the business reason.

## Audience
Primary segment: households within 5 km [assumption] of the bakery that already buy baked goods locally and use Instagram or Facebook. They are reached through local social feeds, local search for bakery terms, and the existing customer list if one exists.
Not targeting: wholesale and corporate catering buyers, and people outside the pickup or delivery area.

## Offer and message
Offer: 10% [assumption] off selected spring items with a promo code, valid 2027-03-01 to 2027-05-31 [assumption]. Products and terms still to be confirmed by the owner.
Message: Spring treats, 10% [assumption] off this season at the bakery.
Proof: No product facts were supplied. The owner should provide ingredients, sourcing or other facts before any product claim is published. None is made in this brief.
Call to action: Order with the spring promo code (code to be chosen by the owner).

## Channel plan
| Channel | Role | Budget | KPI | Target | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) | Create demand in the local area | $800 [assumption] | Promo-code orders from the Meta link | 30 orders [assumption] | Owner (to confirm) |
| Local search ads (Google) | Capture people already searching | $500 [assumption] | Promo-code orders from search | 20 orders [assumption] | Owner (to confirm) |
| Email to existing customers | Convert existing customers | $0 (owned list) [assumption] | Promo-code orders from email | 10 orders [assumption] | Owner (to confirm) |
| Reserve | Move money to the channel that works | $200 [assumption] | n/a | n/a | Owner (to confirm) |

Channel targets add up to 60 orders [calc: 30 + 20 + 10], the primary objective.

## Budget
Total budget: $1,500 [given]
Split reasoning: most money goes to local Meta ads for the widest local reach on a seasonal offer. Local search ads come next to catch people already looking. Email to existing customers costs nothing beyond existing tools. A reserve of $200 [assumption] (13% [calc: 200 / 1,500]) moves money toward whichever channel performs.
Included: media spend only.
Excluded: staff time, the cost of the discount itself (10% [assumption] off margin), photography or design, and any tool subscriptions.

## Measurement
| KPI | Data source | Checked | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promo-code orders, total | Order records from the bakery's order or till system (source to confirm) | Weekly | If total orders are below 10 [assumption] after week 4, review the offer and the message before adding spend. |
| Cost per order, Meta | Ads platform report, matched against order records | Weekly | Target cost per order is $26.67 [calc: 800 / 30]. If cost per order is above $40 [assumption] after $400 [assumption] spent, pause the weakest ad set and move money to email. |
| Cost per order, search | Search ads report, matched against order records | Weekly | Target cost per order is $25.00 [calc: 500 / 20]. If cost per order is above $50 [assumption] after $250 [assumption] spent, pause the weakest ad group and shift money to the reserve. |

Path from money to objective, one line per step:
Meta: assumed cost per click $1.50 [assumption] gives 533 clicks [calc: 800 / 1.50]; 10% click-to-order [assumption] gives 53 orders [calc: 533 x 0.10].
Search: assumed cost per click $2.00 [assumption] gives 250 clicks [calc: 500 / 2.00]; 15% click-to-order [assumption] gives 37 orders [calc: 250 x 0.15].
Email: 10 orders [assumption], since no list size was supplied.
Sum: 100 orders [calc: 53 + 37 + 10] against a target of 60. The margin covers lower rates than assumed, but none of these rates is measured yet.

## Risks and guardrails
1. Demand is lower than assumed. Early sign: fewer than 5 orders in week 2 [assumption]. Response: check the offer and message, then move money from Meta to email.
2. Ads cost more per click than assumed. Early sign: cost per click above $2.50 [assumption] in the first week. Response: narrow the audience and shift money to email.
3. The discount lowers margin without adding orders. Early sign: average order value falls. Response: limit the offer to selected items.
4. Product and price claims must be substantiated by the owner before publishing. Words such as "fresh" or "best" need evidence.
5. Ad platform rules and local rules on food promotions apply. This brief is not a legal review.
