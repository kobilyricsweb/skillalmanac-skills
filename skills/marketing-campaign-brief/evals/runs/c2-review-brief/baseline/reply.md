# Review of "Campaign brief: Q1 demand push (v3 - for CEO sign-off)"

Verdict: do not send to the CEO yet. The budget does not add up, the lead target is about five times what the stated funnel assumptions can produce, and the headline claim ("Guaranteed 3x ROAS") is one the brief cannot support and that a CEO should not approve as written.

## 1. Arithmetic errors (fix first)

- **Budget does not sum.** Channel budgets are $12,000 + $8,000 + $6,500 + $4,000 = **$30,500**. The brief says the total is **$28,000**. The $2,500 gap is unexplained.
- **Lead targets do not match the stated funnel math.** The Measurement section assumes CPC $4 and landing page conversion 2%. That gives a cost per lead of $4 / 0.02 = **$200**.
  - Paid search: $12,000 / $200 = **60 leads**. Target is **300**.
  - LinkedIn: $8,000 / $200 = **40 leads**. Target is **150**.
  - Paid total: about **100 leads** from $20,000. Target is **450**.
  - To get 300 search leads at 2% conversion, the CPC would have to be about $0.80, not $4.
  - To reach 500 leads at these assumptions, you need 25,000 clicks, which is about **$100,000** of paid media, not $28,000.
- **Objective vs. channel totals do not agree.** The objective says 500 leads. Channel targets are 300 + 150 = 450 leads, plus 400 webinar registrations. If registrations count, the plan implies 850 "leads". If they do not, the plan is 50 short of the objective before any analysis of feasibility.

## 2. Measurement section (the assumptions are wrong or unsupported)

- **CTR of 4.5% is applied to every channel.** A blended 4.5% is a plausible search benchmark, but it is not a reasonable figure for LinkedIn ads, where CTRs are typically well below 1%. From my own knowledge, not a sourced figure, so verify before the CEO sees it. The brief also never uses CTR in any calculation, so it adds nothing as written.
- **CPC of $4 is applied to LinkedIn.** LinkedIn CPCs are generally well above search CPCs. Using $4 likely overstates LinkedIn click volume. Again, check against current account data.
- **No source for any benchmark.** The brief says "industry average" but gives no source, date, or vertical. The CEO cannot tell whether these are the company's own numbers or generic ones.
- **No baseline.** There is no current conversion rate, CPC, or lead volume for this company, so the targets cannot be sanity-checked against past performance.
- **"Track performance in the dashboard" is not a measurement plan.** It names no dashboard, no owner, no attribution model, no UTM convention, and no definition of a "lead" versus a "registration" versus a "demo booked". Those definitions decide whether the 500 target is met.
- **No ROAS model.** "Guaranteed 3x ROAS" needs revenue per lead or per customer, lead-to-close rate, and average deal value. None are given. At $200 per lead, 3x ROAS means about $600 of revenue per lead, which the brief never supports.

## 3. The message and the claims

- **"Guaranteed 3x ROAS"** is a promise the company cannot control. Remove it, or replace it with a modelled target that is labelled as a target. A guarantee to a CEO-approved external claim is also a legal and reputational risk.
- **"The #1 scheduling tool"** needs a named, dated source (market share, analyst ranking, or similar) or it should not be used. Superlatives like this are regulated or challenged in advertising in both the US (FTC) and the UK (ASA/CAP).
- **"Cuts no-shows in half"** needs evidence: a study, customer data, or a case study, with the sample and timeframe. Without it, it is an unsupported efficacy claim for both markets.
- The brief does not say whether copy has been checked by legal or compliance.

## 4. Audience and offer

- **Two audiences with one message.** SMB owners (25-60, US and UK) and enterprise ops leaders are very different buyers. The brief does not say which channel reaches which audience, and there is no enterprise-specific offer or message.
- **Three calls to action.** "Book a demo", "Start free", and "Download the guide" serve different funnel stages. The brief does not say which is primary, so the paid campaigns cannot be measured cleanly.
- **No geographic split.** US and UK are both named, but there is no budget, language, currency, or compliance split between them.

## 5. Channel plan

- **Podcast sponsorship ($6,500) has no KPI, no target, and no measure.** It is labelled "Awareness", but awareness is not measured anywhere in the brief. The objective is "brand awareness and 500 leads", so one of the two goals has no metric.
- **Webinar target is registrations, not leads.** It is not clear whether the 400 registrations count toward the 500 leads, or how many will attend. Registrations are usually a weak proxy for pipeline.
- **Owners.** Rae owns both paid channels, Tom owns podcast and webinar. No one owns the landing pages, the tracking setup, the dashboard, or the legal review.
- **No allocation logic.** The brief does not explain why paid search gets $12,000 and LinkedIn $8,000, or why the podcast is funded before the lead target is proven achievable.

## 6. Timeline and decision

- **Year is not stated.** "Q1" and "January to March" are ambiguous. Given today's date (2026-11-20), this is presumably Q1 2027, but the brief should say so.
- **No dates for the dependencies.** Landing pages, creative, tracking, and webinar date are not scheduled. There is no approval deadline and no ramp-up period, which matters for paid search and LinkedIn learning phases.
- **No decision requested.** The brief does not say what the CEO is being asked to approve (budget, targets, or both), or what happens if the targets are missed.
- **No risks or fallback.** There is no section on what happens if CPC is higher than assumed or if conversion is lower.

## 7. What I would change before sending

1. Reconcile the budget to one number ($28,000 or $30,500) and show the split.
2. Rebuild the lead forecast from the funnel formula, using sourced or company-specific CPC, CTR, and conversion for each channel. Show whether 500 leads is reachable at this budget. At the stated assumptions it is not.
3. Either lower the lead target to what the budget supports, or raise the budget, and say which.
4. Define "lead", "registration", and "demo" and state which count toward the 500.
5. Give the podcast a measurable KPI, such as brand search lift or branded traffic, with a baseline.
6. Remove "Guaranteed 3x ROAS". Replace it with a modelled ROAS range, with its inputs listed.
7. Source or remove "#1" and "cuts no-shows in half", and have legal review the claims for the US and UK.
8. Pick one primary CTA per audience and state which channel serves SMB and which serves enterprise.
9. Name the dashboard, its owner, the attribution method, and the UTM convention.
10. State the year, the approval deadline, the dependency dates, and the decision the CEO is being asked to make.

## Limits of this review

- I used only the brief. I did not have account data, prior campaign results, the company's own conversion or revenue figures, or any benchmark source. The industry-benchmark comments (LinkedIn CTR and CPC ranges) are from general knowledge and should be checked against current data.
- The arithmetic in sections 1 and 2 uses only numbers stated in the brief and is exact.
