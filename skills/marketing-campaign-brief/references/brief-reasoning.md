# Reasoning behind a campaign brief

Read when choosing channels, building the path from budget to target, or writing decision rules.

## Contents

1. Objective patterns
2. Audience: one segment
3. Channel roles

## 1. Objective patterns

| Weak | Workable |
|---|---|
| "Increase awareness" | "Aided awareness among the segment from 12% to 20% by 30 June, read from the quarterly survey" |
| "Get more leads" | "Qualified demo requests from 40 to 70 a month by the end of Q2, read from the CRM" |
| "Boost sales" | "First orders from new customers from 310 to 450 in the 6 campaign weeks, read from the shop back end" |
| "Grow the list" | "Newsletter subscribers from 8,200 to 10,000 by 1 September, read from the email tool" |

When there is no baseline, say so and make the first two weeks a baseline period with a provisional target. When the business goal is revenue but the campaign can only move an earlier step, name the step the campaign owns and show the link to revenue as `[calc]` or `[assumption]`.

## 2. Audience: one segment

A segment is usable when it names a situation, not a demographic: "owners of restaurants with 1 to 3 sites who still take bookings by phone" beats "SMB owners 30 to 55". Write where they already pay attention (a trade newsletter, a search query, a marketplace category, a local group) because that decides the channels. A second segment is a second campaign or a later phase.

## 3. Channel roles

Give each channel exactly one job.

| Role | Typical channels | KPI family |
|---|---|---|
| Capture existing demand | search ads, marketplace listings, comparison sites, retargeting | cost per lead or order, conversion rate |
| Create demand | social video, creators, display, out-of-home, PR | reach in segment, visits from new users, branded search |
| Convert and nurture | email, landing page, sales follow-up, webinars | reply or booking rate, lead-to-order rate |
| Retain and expand | email to customers, in-product messages, referral | repeat rate, referrals |

Rules of thumb, to be stated as reasoning and not as fact about the user's market: a short campaign with a small budget and a sales target leans on capturing demand and owned channels; creating demand needs enough repetition to be noticed, so a thin spread across many channels usually does less than two channels done properly; a channel the team has never run needs a test budget and a learning goal, not a sales target.

## 4. Funnel arithmetic

Write the path from money to the objective, one line per step, each number tagged:

```
Budget for search ads: $4,000 [given]
Assumed cost per click: $2.50 [assumption] -> 1,600 clicks [calc: 4,000 / 2.50]
Landing page conversion to lead: 4% [given: last quarter's average] -> 64 leads [calc: 1,600 x 0.04]
Lead to order: 25% [assumption] -> 16 orders [calc]
```

Then compare the sum across channels with the target. If the arithmetic falls short, say so plainly and offer the levers: more budget, a longer window, a lower target, a better rate (with what would have to be true). Never tune an assumption until the target is met. Where the user has past data, use it and tag it `[given]`; where they have none, give a range from a cautious and a hopeful assumption, and make the first review date the moment the assumption is replaced by a real number.

## 5. Budget split

- Start from the roles: fund the channel closest to the objective first.
- Keep a reserve (often around a tenth) to move toward what works; show it as a row so the table still adds up.
- Separate media spend from production, tools and fees, and say which the total includes.
- State what is not in the budget: staff time, discounts given, agency retainers.

## 6. Decision rules

Each KPI gets a rule with a threshold, a date and an action:

- "If cost per lead from search is above $80 after the first $1,000, pause the two weakest ad groups and move the money to email."
- "If landing-page conversion is under 2% after 500 visits, change the page before buying more traffic."
- "If the survey shows awareness unchanged at the mid-point, stop the display buy and keep creators."

A rule needs a sample large enough to mean something; say what that is, or tag it `[assumption]`.

## 7. Review questions for an existing brief

1. Is there one primary objective with metric, baseline, target and date?
2. Can the channel targets add up to it? Do the arithmetic.
3. Do the channel budgets equal the total?
4. Which figures have no stated origin?
5. Does each KPI have a data source that exists today?
6. Is the audience one reachable segment?
7. Is there one offer and one call to action?
8. What would make the team stop? If nothing, the brief has no decision rule.
9. Which product claims need substantiation?
10. What is the single assumption that, if wrong, sinks the plan?
