# Reply shapes

Read the section that matches the case. Names, items and terms in the examples are placeholders for illustration; real replies take every term from the user's policy.

## When the policy says yes

Lead with the yes. Then the steps, then the time the policy states.

> Hi Jordan,
> I'm sorry the kettle arrived cracked. You can choose a replacement or a refund, whichever you prefer.
> 1. Reply with a photo of the damage.
> 2. Tell me which option you want.
> Once we have the photo, we'll email you a prepaid return label. [Then the time frame, in the policy's own words.]

Do not add extras the policy does not list (a discount, free shipping on the next order).

## When the policy says no

Say no once, plainly, in the same sentence as what is possible. Give the reason in the policy's terms without quoting clause numbers.

> Because the order was delivered more than 30 days ago, I can't refund it to your card. What I can do is [the alternative the policy allows].

Avoid: "Unfortunately, as stated in our policy...", "I understand your frustration, however...", a second or third apology, and any hint that an exception is likely.

## When the answer is mixed

Most real tickets are mixed: one request is a yes, one is a no, one needs approval. Put the yes first, the no second with its alternative, the pending item last with who is looking at it.

## When it needs a human

The policy is silent, or it says a supervisor decides. The reply states three things: that the request has been passed on, to whom (a role, not a personal name unless supplied), and what the customer should expect next. If the policy gives no time for the review, do not give one.

> I've passed your request for a goodwill credit to our support supervisor, who decides these. I can't confirm the outcome myself, and I'll write to you as soon as I have their answer.

In the internal note write exactly what must be decided and what the customer was told.

## When a fact is missing

Ask for it before answering the part that depends on it. One question, specific: "Could you tell me the delivery date shown in your confirmation email?" Answer everything that does not depend on it in the same reply.

## When the customer quotes a promise the company did not make

Do not argue and do not confirm it. State what the policy does provide. Record the claim in the internal note so a human can check the website or advert the customer saw.

## Tone rules

- Plain words, short sentences, the customer's name once.
- Match formality to the customer's message, one step calmer.
- Angry customer: acknowledge the specific failure in one sentence, then move straight to the fix. Do not mirror the anger or defend the company.
- No "we value your business", "rest assured", or "please do not hesitate".
- State times the way the policy states them ("5 to 7 business days after we receive the return"), including the starting point.
- End with the single next action, not a paragraph of courtesy.

## Checklist before handing over

1. Every question in the ticket has an answer or a stated hand-off.
2. Every number in the reply is in the policy or the ticket.
3. Nothing commits another team to an outcome.
4. Day counts are written in the internal note.
5. The reply could be sent as it stands, with no brackets left to fill.
