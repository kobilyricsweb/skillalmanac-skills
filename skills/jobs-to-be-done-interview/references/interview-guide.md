## 1. Recruiting screen

Ask by email or form before booking, three questions at most:

1. "What did you start using, or stop using, for [job area] in the last three months?" (names the event)
2. "Roughly which date did you decide?" (must be inside 90 days; 60 is better)
3. "What were you using before, or doing instead?" (confirms a real alternative, including nothing)

Accept: a dated decision, made by the person you will interview (not delegated). Reject: "I might", "my team lead chose it", decisions older than 120 days. Aim for a mix: about two thirds adopters, one third people who tried and stopped or chose a rival. Incentive is paid regardless of what they say.

## 2. The 55-minute timeline

| Phase | Min | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Context | 5 | Who they are in this story; consent; recording |
| 2 First thought | 10 | The first moment the old way stopped being fine |
| 3 Passive looking | 8 | Noticing options without acting |
| 4 Active looking and deciding | 12 | Comparing, who else was involved, what tipped it |
| 5 First use | 8 | Purchase day, set-up, first result |
| 6 Ongoing use | 8 | What changed in their days, what they kept of the old way |
| 7 Close | 4 | Anything missed, thanks, no pitch |

Anchor the whole interview on one date: "the day you decided". Walk back to the first thought, then forward to use. If the participant jumps, follow them and note the turn numbers; the timeline is a coverage checklist, not a script order.

## 3. Prompts and listening notes per phase

**1 Context.** "Tell me a little about your role and who else is involved in [area]." Do not ask about the product.

**2 First thought.** "Think back to the first time you wondered whether there was a better way. Where were you? What was going on?" "What happened right before that?" Listen for a dated event, a person who complained, a cost they could name. This is the main source of push.

**3 Passive looking.** "After that, what did you do or notice?" "Did anyone mention anything?" Listen for what they saw, heard or saved and did not act on. Source of early pull, and of habit ("I kept using the old way for a few weeks").

**4 Active looking and deciding.** "When did it go from noticing to actually looking?" "What else did you consider, including doing nothing?" "Who else had a say?" "What was the moment you decided?" Listen for the tipping event, the deadline, and the doubts. Source of pull and anxiety.

**5 First use.** "Take me through the first day you used it." "What did you expect, and what happened?" Listen for the first result they cared about; it often differs from the reason they gave for buying.

**6 Ongoing use.** "What is different in your week now?" "What from the old way do you still do?" Listen for habit that survived and outcomes in their words.

**7 Close.** "Is there something about this decision I did not ask about?" Stop recording only after thanks.

## 4. Probing each force

Probe whenever a force has not been heard after phase 5. Use their wording.

- **Push**: "You said it was [their word]. When did that last happen?" Ask for the most recent episode, not a general statement.
- **Pull**: "What did you picture would be different?" "Where did you first see it working?" Seeing it in someone else's hands is strong evidence.
- **Anxiety**: "Before you committed, what were you unsure about?" "Who did you check with?" "What would have made you walk away?"
- **Habit**: "What was good enough about the old way?" "What did you have to give up, or learn again?"

If a force stays silent after two neutral probes, record `none_found` with the probes you used. Do not push a third time; that creates the answer.

## 5. Rewrites for common bad questions

| Bad | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "Wouldn't you say onboarding is too slow?" | Tag question, supplies the answer | "Take me through your first day." |
| "Would you pay 20 a month for this?" | Hypothetical price | "What did you pay before, and when did you last pay for it?" |
| "Do you like the new dashboard?" | Opinion about the product | "What did you do the last time you opened it?" |
| "Why did you leave the old tool?" | Why-prompt, invites a tidy reason | "What was going on when you stopped using it?" |
| "Was it the price or the speed?" | Offers two answers | "What tipped it?" |
| "What features do you want?" | Solution request | "What did you do last time you needed that?" |
| "Tell me about your workflow, and which tools you use, and what you hate" | Three questions in one | Ask one, wait, ask the next |

## 6. Closing and consent

Open by saying: the interview is recorded, notes are used for the study only, they can skip any question, and nothing here is a sales call. Keep names out of the study file: use P1, P2. Delete recordings on the date you promised.
