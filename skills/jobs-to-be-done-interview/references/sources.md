Where the method comes from, in my own words. These pages were not re-fetched while this skill was written; the summaries come from the authoring model's prior knowledge and should be checked against the pages before anything here is quoted publicly.

- Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done" (Harvard Business Review, 2016): https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done . Argues that people hire products to make progress in a particular circumstance, so research should start from the circumstance and not from demographics. Source of the idea that the situation is the unit of study.
- Jobs-to-be-Done.info, Alan Klement's site: https://jtbd.info/ . Describes the forces of progress (push of the situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety, habit) and the "When ..., I want to ..., so I can ..." statement format used here. The numeric values in this skill (90 days, 70%, 6 to 10 interviews) are the skill's own working values, not taken from it.
- The Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick: https://www.momtestbook.com/ . Rules for customer conversations: ask about past behaviour, not future intentions; avoid mentioning your idea; opinions and compliments are not evidence. Source of the hypothetical and solution-priming rules.
- The timeline of a purchase (first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding, first use, ongoing use) is the interview structure taught by the Re-Wired group, founded by Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek: https://rewired.group/ . This skill gives its own phase budgets in minutes.
