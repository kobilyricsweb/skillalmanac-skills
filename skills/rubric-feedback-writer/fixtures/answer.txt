Photosynthesis begins when chlorophyll in the thylakoid membranes absorbs light, mostly red and blue wavelengths, while green light is reflected, which is why leaves look green. In the light-dependent reactions, light energy splits water molecules, releasing oxygen as a byproduct, and the process produces ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle then uses these molecules to fix carbon dioxide into sugar, with the enzyme RuBisCO catalyzing the first step. This matters for ecosystems because the oxygen supports animal respiration, and the sugars made by plants start nearly every food chain. Plants and algae therefore sit at the base of most food webs. Scientists often measure this process by tracking how much carbon dioxide a leaf takes in steadily under bright light.
