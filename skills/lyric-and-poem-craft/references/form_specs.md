## Form table

These are the working targets the script checks. They are conventions, not laws; a deliberate break is fine when the author says so.

| Form | Shape | Rhyme | Syllable target the script checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| verse-chorus | verses, chorus repeated at least twice, optional pre-chorus and bridge | verses often ABAB or AABB; chorus often AABB or ABAB | none per line; consistency inside a section |
| AABA song form | four sections: A, A, B (bridge), A, same length for each A | A sections share one scheme; B differs | each A ends on the refrain or title line |
| ballad stanza | quatrains | lines 2 and 4 rhyme (ABCB) or ABAB | 8-6-8-6 (three or four beats per line) |
| Shakespearean sonnet | 14 lines: three quatrains and a couplet | ABAB CDCD EFEF GG | about 10 per line (accepts 9 to 11) |
| Petrarchan sonnet | 14 lines: octave then sestet | ABBAABBA then CDECDE or CDCDCD | about 10 per line |
| haiku | 3 lines | usually none | 5-7-5 as a count; English haiku often loosen it |
| limerick | 5 lines | AABBA | lines 1, 2, 5 about 7 to 10; lines 3, 4 about 5 to 7 |
| rap bars | verse of bars (one line per bar), hook of 4 to 8 bars | end rhyme plus internal and multi-syllable rhyme | 8 to 16 verse bars; mean 8 to 16 syllables per bar |

## Section length table (songs)

Lines per section. "Usual" is the range the script treats as normal; "hard" is the range outside which it raises a warning.

| Section | Usual | Hard limits | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| verse | 4 to 8 | 2 to 12 | two verses should differ by fewer than 2 lines |
| chorus | 4 to 8 | 2 to 10 | must come back; later choruses stay near-identical |
| pre-chorus | 2 to 4 | 1 to 6 | lifts into the chorus |
| bridge | 2 to 6 (4 typical) | 1 to 8 | shows up after the second chorus, not before the first |
| intro, outro | 1 to 4 | 1 to 8 | |

Delay to first chorus: more than 16 lyric lines before it is flagged, because the hook arrives late.

## Rhyme types and when to use them

| Type | What matches | Use it for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| perfect | vowel sound and everything after it (night / light) | chorus ends, closing couplets | very common pairs sound predictable |
| slant (near) | same vowel with different ending, or same ending with different vowel (heart / hurt, cat / cut) | verses, modern or serious tone, avoiding sing-song | too many in a row reads as unrhymed |
| identical | the same word again | refrains, deliberate echo | reads as a lazy rhyme if accidental |
| internal | two words inside a line, or mid-line to line end | rap, momentum, tongue-twisting lines | heavy use tires the ear |
| feminine | stressed syllable plus unstressed one (running / stunning) | comic or tender lines | the script cannot hear stress; it treats the suffix as shared |

## Hook placement and repetition

- The title phrase should appear 2 to 4 times in each chorus; once is easy to miss, more than 4 starts to grate.
- Put the title in the first or last line of the chorus; both positions are remembered.
- A chorus repeated later should match the first line for line unless the change is on purpose (a final chorus that lifts one line is a normal move).
- A line repeated three or more times inside one verse is a refrain; mark it as intended.

## Sources

Read in 2026 and restated in the author's own words above:

- https://poets.org/glossary/sonnet : fourteen lines, usually iambic pentameter; Petrarchan octave and sestet; Shakespearean three quatrains and a couplet.
- https://poets.org/glossary/limerick : five lines, first, second and fifth rhyme, shorter third and fourth rhyme.
- https://poets.org/glossary/haiku : three lines, traditionally 5-7-5 in Japanese; the pattern is often broken in practice.
- https://poets.org/glossary/ballad : four-line stanzas, three or four stresses a line, second and fourth lines rhyme or alternate rhyme.

The song-section ranges and the rap bar ranges are working conventions chosen for this skill, not measured from a corpus.
