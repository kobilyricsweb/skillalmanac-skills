The evening folds its coat across the hill
and lamps wake up along the river's edge
the market stalls grow quiet, then grow still
while swallows stitch their shadows to the ledge

I carry questions in my pockets, small
like pebbles that I never learned to name
and none of them is answered by the wall
I lean against, awaiting some small flame

So count the hours gently, one by one
and thank the lamps for choosing to stay
and let the tired heart rest beneath the sun
then watch the evening turn and slip away

What small thing ever lasted? Only this:
A quiet window and an evening's kiss
