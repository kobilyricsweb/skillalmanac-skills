Sources behind the rules, in my own words. They were cited from the author's knowledge and were not re-fetched when this skill was written; check them before relying on the exact claims. The numeric limits in the skill are working values, not figures from these papers.

- Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999), "Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans", American Psychologist 54(7). https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493 . Reviews studies in which people who state when, where and how they will act on a goal follow through more often than people who state only the goal. This supports naming the object, the place and the day in each step.
- Buehler, R., Griffin, D., and Ross, M. (1994), "Exploring the planning fallacy", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 67(3). https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366 . Reports that people underestimate how long their own tasks take even when they know similar past tasks ran late. This supports the 50% allowance for unfamiliar work and the 70% day-load target.
- The "next action" idea (the single physical step that moves a project, as distinct from the project itself) comes from the personal-productivity method of David Allen. No page is cited; the skill's rule in step 5 is its own wording.
