# Question banks by interview type

Open only the section for the interview at hand. For each question the second column says what a strong answer must contain, so the agent can rehearse and give feedback against it. Stories come from the user's bank only.

## Recruiter screen (20 to 30 minutes)

| Question | A strong answer contains |
|---|---|
| Walk me through your background. | 90 seconds, present to past, ending on why this role; no full CV recital. |
| Why are you looking? | A reason pointing toward something, not away from the current employer. |
| Why this company? | One fact the user has read, tied to one of their must-haves. |
| What are your salary expectations? | A range anchored on the pay floor in the criteria sheet; or a polite request for the band first. State this and let the user decide; no negotiation advice beyond that. |
| When can you start, and any constraints? | Notice period, location or visa facts the user supplied. |

## Hiring manager

| Question | A strong answer contains |
|---|---|
| What would you do in the first 90 days? | Listen, learn the systems, one small deliverable; tied to a problem the post names. |
| Tell me about a project you are proud of. | A story with a measurable result and the user's own part. |
| How do you prioritise when everything is urgent? | A concrete rule and one example. |
| What kind of manager brings out your best work? | Specific behaviours, not adjectives. |

## Behavioural round

Use the story bank. Typical prompts by theme: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague" (conflict); "a time you failed" (failure); "a time you led without formal authority" (leadership); "a deadline you almost missed" (pressure); "a decision with incomplete information" (ambiguity). For each, check the story has a first-person action and a result, and that the user can answer two follow-ups: "What would you do differently?" and "What did the other person think?"

## Technical or skills round

| Format | Preparation steps |
|---|---|
| Live exercise in the user's tools | List the 5 core skills in the post; practise one timed exercise per skill; rehearse narrating decisions aloud. |
| Take-home task | Ask for the time limit and what is assessed; state assumptions in writing; submit what is finished and say what is not. |
| Case or scenario | Clarify the goal, state the structure before calculating, give a recommendation first and then the reasoning, name one risk. |

Never fill gaps in the user's technical knowledge by writing their answers for a live test; coach the explanation instead.

## Panel

Ask for the panel's names and roles in advance (the user may ask the recruiter). Prepare one question per panelist's function. In the room: address the person who asked, include the others with eye contact, and keep each answer under 2 minutes.

## Final or senior round

Expect strategy and judgement questions: "Where do you see this function in two years?", "What would worry you about this role?", "Tell me about a time you changed your mind." Prepare a one-page view of the company's likely problems from public sources the user has read; label inferences as inferences.

## Questions the user asks

Pick 4 and tailor to the interviewer: how success is measured in the first six months; what the team found hardest in the last quarter; how decisions are made when the team disagrees; what the next step and timeline are. Avoid questions the job post already answers.
