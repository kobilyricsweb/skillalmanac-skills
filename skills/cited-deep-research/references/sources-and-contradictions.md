# Choosing sources and handling conflicts

## Source tiers

Prefer the tier closest to where the fact was produced.

| Tier | Examples | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Originating | Statute or regulation text, filings, minutes, datasets, the study itself, a company's own release for its own facts, a standard | Settle what was decided, measured or stated |
| 2 Independent analysis | Peer-reviewed review, audit, statistical agency commentary, investigative reporting that shows its documents | Settle what it means or whether tier 1 holds up |
| 3 Reporting | News articles, trade press, encyclopedia entries | Locate tier 1; support context |
| 4 Commentary | Opinion, forums, social posts, marketing pages for third-party claims | Show positions and rumours; never settle a fact |

A tier 1 source is still a party: a company release is authoritative for what the company said, not for whether it is true. Mark sources with a stake in the answer and lower confidence one band.

## Independence

Count sources by origin, not by page. Several outlets quoting one study are one origin. A summary page that links to the study counts as the study only after you opened the study. When two sources agree, ask whether one copied the other; if so, treat them as one and say so in the claim row.

## Reading a source for a quote

- Open the page itself, not a cached snippet. Read the sentences around the one you want, because a figure may be qualified a line later ("provisional", "excluding region X").
- Copy the sentence exactly. Cut with an ellipsis only when the omitted words do not change the meaning.
- Note the publication or data date when the page gives one; it is often needed in conflict triage. Put it in the claim text, for example "as of March 2026".
- If the page is long, the quote must still be one you can find again: include enough words to be unique.

## Conflict triage

Work down the list and stop at the first explanation that fits both claims.

1. **Definition.** Different population, unit, geography, or category boundary ("trips" against "commuter trips").
2. **Date.** One figure is older or covers a different period; a later correction supersedes.
3. **Method.** Survey against administrative count; sample against census; estimate against actual.
4. **Tier.** Tier 1 against tier 3 on the same fact: the originating source wins, and the report says the secondary source differs.
5. **Copying.** One source repeated the other's error; count once.
6. **Stake.** One party gains from a higher or lower figure.
7. **Unresolved.** Nothing above explains it. Show both claims side by side with their sources, say what additional evidence would settle it, and keep both confidences at or below 0.6.

Whatever the outcome, the Contradictions table row names the claim ids and gives the explanation in one sentence. A resolved conflict may raise confidence in the winner only as far as its own evidence allows.
