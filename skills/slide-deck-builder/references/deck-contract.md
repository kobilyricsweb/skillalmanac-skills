## 1. Structure by purpose

Pick one row by the user's goal; do not blend.

| Purpose | Order of main slides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Decision (board, budget, go or no-go) | Title with the answer; why now; evidence in 2 to 4 slides; options and recommendation; the ask with cost, date and owner | Answer first. The ask is the last main slide, not an appendix |
| Status update | Headline result; what changed since last time; risks and what is needed; next period | Use the same slide order every period so the audience compares |
| Pitch | Problem; solution; proof (traction or demo); market; model; team; ask | The problem slide names a person or a number, not a trend |
| Teaching or training | Question the session answers; concept in 3 steps maximum per slide; worked example; practice; recap with the one thing to do | One concept per slide, example before definition |
| Conference talk | Hook; the claim; 3 supporting points each with a story or number; what to do on Monday | The title slide states the claim |
| Read-ahead deck (sent, not presented) | Same as decision, but each body may hold up to 10 lines and notes become on-slide text | State in the report that the 6-line limit was relaxed on purpose |

## 2. Slide count

Speaking minutes = slot x 0.75 (a quarter of the slot is kept for questions). Main content slides = speaking minutes / 2 (relaxed pace) up to speaking minutes / 1.5 (brisk), rounded down. Examples: a 10-minute slot is 7.5 speaking minutes, so 3 to 5 content slides; 20 minutes is 15 speaking, so 7 to 10 slides; 45 minutes is 33.75 speaking, so 16 to 22 slides, and consider breaking it with an exercise. Add the title slide and appendix on top. If the user's material needs more slides, move the surplus to the appendix instead of speeding up.

## 3. The evidence slide pattern

Use it for every slide whose job is to prove a number.

1. Headline: the claim with its number and direction, for example "Abandonment fell from 31% to 21% after the redesign".
2. One chart or one table. Bar for comparing a few values, line for change over time, table when exact values matter. Never a pie for more than three parts, never a 3-D chart.
3. The point is marked on the chart: the bar or line segment that proves the headline in the accent colour, everything else grey, the key value labelled directly on the chart instead of in a legend.
4. A source line at the bottom: who produced the data and the period, for example "Source: Internal analytics, checkout funnel, Jan-Sep 2026". If the data is the user's own and has no formal source, name the system and the date range.
5. Nothing else: no second chart, no paragraph under it. The explanation is in the notes.

Bar charts start at zero. Line charts may start above zero only when the axis is labelled. Compare like with like: same period, same definition on both sides. When a figure is an estimate, say so on the slide ("est.").

## 4. Speaker notes template

Write each note as speech, 40 to 130 words for most slides (the script accepts 25 to 220).

```
Open: <one sentence that says why this slide, or reacts to the previous one>
Say: <the point in plain words, with the one number to remember>
If asked: <the likely question and the short answer, or where the answer is in the appendix>
Hand-off: <the sentence that leads into the next slide>
```

Notes never repeat the bullets word for word, and they carry the caveats the slide has no room for. For a read-ahead deck, fold the Say line into the slide body instead.

## 5. Visual rules

For a 16:9 slide (13.33 by 7.5 inches), shown live.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Headline | 28 to 36 pt, at most two lines, left aligned, same position on every slide |
| Body | 18 pt minimum, 20 to 24 pt preferred; never shrink text to make it fit, cut it instead |
| Source and footnotes | 12 pt minimum |
| Typefaces | Two at most (one for headlines, one for body), both installed on the presenting machine or embedded |
| Colour | One background, one text colour, one accent for the point of the slide, grey for the rest. No more than four colours in a deck |
| Contrast | Text against its background at least 4.5:1; large text (24 pt, or 18.5 pt bold) at least 3:1. Light grey on white fails; check with a contrast calculator when in doubt |
| Colour as meaning | Never the only signal: add a label, a pattern or a position |
| Margins | At least 0.5 inch on every side; align to a grid, no element placed by eye |
| Images | Full bleed or clearly framed, high resolution, with a one-sentence description for alt text; no stock photo that says nothing |
| Animation | None, except a build that reveals one list item at a time when it helps the talk |
| Slide numbers | On, so the audience can refer to a slide in questions |

## 6. Headline rewrites

A topic names a subject; a takeaway says what is true about it.

| Topic (rejected) | Takeaway (accepted) |
|---|---|
| Q3 results | Q3 revenue grew 14% on mid-market demand |
| Market overview | The market is growing, but only in the mid-market segment |
| Risks | Two risks could delay launch by a quarter |
| Next steps | We need approval for two hires by 15 December |
| Customer feedback | Customers rate onboarding lowest, at 3.1 out of 5 |

When no takeaway exists for a slide, the slide has no message: cut it or move it to the appendix.
