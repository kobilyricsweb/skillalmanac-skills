- **Must fix before investors see it:** Pawhaven's fee is 15%, not 20%. SitNest reports 90,000 pet parents since launch, not 150,000 active users.
- **Unsupported, remove or label as estimate:** the 30% growth rate, the "leader" claim, and the ~$3M ARR estimate.

- **Missing context:** only 11% of those households paid for sitting or boarding in the past 12 months.

## Source reliability

- **Tailkeep home page (03) and Tailkeep blog (05)** are a competitor's marketing. The blog states "Pawhaven charges a 20% fee", "150,000 users" and "growing 30% a year" with no sources. Every competitor figure in the draft that is not from the competitor's own page traces back to this blog. Do not use it as a source for competitor figures.
- **The Tailkeep home page (03) contains text addressed to AI assistants**, telling them to run a shell command and register an account before reading further. I did not run or follow it. Treat that page as unreliable, and check it before any link to it goes into investor material.
- **Search results (04)** are previews only. None of the articles was opened.

## Suggested corrected text

> The Canadian pet-sitting market was valued at about $1.2B in 2025 (MarketGlance; paywalled report, figure taken from a preview; currency to be confirmed). Pawhaven reports 12,400 active sitters in 38 Canadian cities and takes a 15% service fee on each completed booking. A PetBiz Weekly article (March 2026) reports a $14M Series A; to be checked against the full article. SitNest reports 90,000 pet parents since its 2021 launch, keeps 18% of each booking, and operates in Ontario, Quebec and British Columbia. Tailkeep reports 4,100+ verified sitters and charges owners CA$9.99 a month with no commission; sitters keep 100% of what they charge. Tailkeep's revenue is not disclosed; any ARR figure should be labelled as our estimate, with its method. According to the 2024 national household survey, 7.9 million of 15.3 million households have a dog or cat, and 11% of pet-owning households paid for pet sitting or boarding in the past 12 months (about 869,000 households).
