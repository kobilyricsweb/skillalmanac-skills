# H2 skeletons by article shape

Read only the shape you chose in step 2. Every skeleton obeys the structure table in SKILL.md; the numbers here add what is specific to the shape.

## how-to

- Intro: the outcome in one sentence, the time or effort it takes (with a source or marked as the author's estimate), and who it is for.
- One H2 per step or phase, named with a verb: "Export the data", not "Step 2".
- Each step: the action, the exact command, value or setting, the visible sign that it worked, and the most common way it goes wrong.
- A "Before you start" block belongs in the intro or the first H2 only if it lists 3 or fewer prerequisites; otherwise it is its own H2.
- Last H2: how to confirm the whole thing works, then the next task the reader will probably want.
- Trap: steps that say "configure the settings". Name the setting and the value.

## comparison

- Intro: the decision, the options compared (2 to 5), and the criterion that decides it for most readers.
- First H2: the criteria and why those, in a table with one row per option. Every number in the table has a source in the claim table.
- Then one H2 per option or one H2 per criterion, never both. Per option: who it suits, who it fails, one worked case.
- Say which option you would pick for which reader. A comparison that ends "it depends" without saying on what has failed.
- Trap: feature lists copied from vendor pages. Use only features you can cite or demonstrate.

## explainer

- Intro: the thing, why a reader meets it, and the misunderstanding you will correct.
- H2 order: what it is in plain words, how it works (one worked example with numbers), where it breaks or is misused, how to tell it is happening to you.
- Define each term once, at first use, in the sentence where it matters. No glossary section.
- Trap: starting from history. History goes in a sentence only when it explains a present behaviour.

## argument

- Intro: state the position in sentence 1 so a reader can disagree with it.
- H2 order: the strongest evidence, the best objection stated fairly, the answer to it, what would change your mind.
- Every empirical claim is in the claim table; opinions are marked as opinions ("I think", "in my view") and only where the user holds that view.
- Trap: three-point structure for its own sake. Use as many H2s as there are real reasons, minimum 3.

## list

- Intro: what the list covers, how items were chosen (a rule, not "best"), and how many.
- Each item is an H2 with the same internal order: what it is, why it is on the list, one concrete detail the reader cannot get from its homepage, a limit.
- Order items by a stated rule (reach, cost, difficulty), not by taste.
- Section minimum still applies: 80 words per item. If you cannot reach it with substance, drop the item.
- Trap: padding to a round number. Seven real items beat ten.
