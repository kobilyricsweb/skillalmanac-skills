# Openers: what to write instead of the stock line

Read this once before writing the intro. The audit script treats the phrases below as errors in an opening paragraph (the intro, and the first paragraph of each H2) and as warnings elsewhere.

## The test

An opening sentence passes if it contains at least one of:

1. a specific number with its source,
2. a named person, product, place or document,
3. a concrete scene the reader will recognise,
4. a claim that a knowledgeable reader could dispute.

If you could paste the sentence into an article on a different topic and it would still read fine, it fails.

## Swaps

| Stock opener | Why it fails | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "In today's fast-paced world, ..." | Says nothing; fits any topic | State the specific change: what happened, when, to whom |
| "Have you ever wondered why ...?" | Asks the reader to care before giving a reason | Give the answer's surprising half first |
| "When it comes to X, there is a lot to consider" | Announces content instead of giving it | Name the one thing to consider first |
| "In this article, we will explore ..." | Narrates the article | The promise, as a claim: "You can cut this to five minutes by ..." |
| "X has become more important than ever" | Unfalsifiable | The number or event that shows it, cited |
| "Whether you are a beginner or an expert" | Refuses to choose an audience | Pick the audience in the brief and name them |
| "Let's dive in" / "Without further ado" | Filler between intro and content | Delete; start the first section |
| "In conclusion" / "To sum up" | Recap nobody needs | End on the next action or the open question |

## Section openers

The first sentence of each H2 states that section's claim. "There are several factors" fails; "Reach, not date, decides the order" passes. If the first sentence can be deleted without loss, delete it.

## Original-sounding does not mean quirky

Do not replace a stock opener with a fake anecdote ("Last Tuesday I was ...") or a rhetorical flourish. Scene openers are allowed only when the scene comes from the user's material or is marked hypothetical ("Suppose a release changes ..."). Plain and specific beats clever.
