**Conclusion: the data do not support calling this skill a proven winner.** The best of 20 candidates, with an unadjusted p of 0.03, is a lead to test, not a result.

**Why**

- **Selection inflates the number.** We picked the largest of 20 observed gains after seeing all of them. Under the null (no skill has any effect), the chance that at least one of 20 reaches p ≤ 0.03 is about 1 − 0.97²⁰ ≈ 46%. A p of 0.03 is what you'd expect from the luckiest of 20 skills that do nothing.
- **Correcting for the 20 comparisons removes the significance.** Bonferroni gives 20 × 0.03 = 0.60. Holm gives the same for the top result. The 19 non-significant results don't rescue it; they confirm the picture is mostly noise.
- **The skill's own rules don't reach LIFT.** LIFT needs a 95% interval entirely above zero and a sign-flip p ≤ 0.05 on tasks the skill was assessed against in advance. Here the skill was chosen after the fact, and I haven't seen the interval, the per-task results, or whether the checks were written before the runs. At best this is LIFT_UNCONFIRMED; the chosen skill's 7 points is probably an overestimate (winner's curse).
- **Other gaps:** 12 tasks is enough for a rough signal, but the runs per arm, grading blindness, and whether all 20 used identical tasks and baselines aren't stated. I can't verify any of this from your summary.
