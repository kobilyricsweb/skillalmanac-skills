# How Plants Make Food

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy, which is absorbed by chlorophyll in the leaves, into chemical energy that is stored in the form of glucose. During this process, carbon dioxide is taken in from the air through tiny openings called stomata, and water is transported upward from the roots through the stem. Oxygen, which is produced as a by-product, is released into the atmosphere, and this is why forests are often described as the lungs of the planet. Scientists estimate that a single large tree can release enough oxygen in one year to supply about 2 people, although the exact amount varies with the species and the climate.

Try this at home: place a green leaf in a clear glass of water in the sun and watch for small bubbles after about 30 minutes.
