## Structure

Five parts, 80 to 180 words:
1. Subject: account name plus the next date, for example "Harbor Logistics follow-up and demo on Nov 19".
2. One sentence of thanks tied to something they said, not generic thanks.
3. Their problem and impact in their own words, one or two sentences, using only figures from the notes.
4. "What we agreed", a short list: each item from Commitments made, with the owner and any date as noted.
5. One ask or a restated next step with date and owner.

## Wording that stays inside the notes

- Their words: "you put the cost at about $5,600 a month", not "you are losing $5,600".
- Their commitments: "you will share two weeks of load data" only if the notes list it under them.
- Something not agreed becomes a question: "Would Thursday Nov 19 work for the demo?" The question mark keeps it from being read as a promise, and the checker skips questions.
- Soften nothing into a promise: "we can probably do" is still a commitment if the notes never said it.

## Case: next step agreed

Restate action, owner, date. Offer to move it, never to cancel it: "If Nov 19 stops working, tell me."

## Case: no next step agreed

Record that in the notes (Next step: action "none agreed", owner and date still required to pass the checker, so propose one). In the email propose exactly one dated step as a question with a date window, for example "Could we use 20 minutes on Nov 24 or Nov 25 to look at your load data?" Update the notes only after they answer.

## Case: "we will think about it"

Ask what the thinking depends on and who else is involved, one question, and offer a dated check-in as a question. Do not add urgency, a deadline, or an incentive the notes lack.

## Case: the call surfaced a question you cannot answer yet

Write: "I do not have that yet; I will check and reply by <date>". That is a commitment, so put it in Commitments made first, with the date, then in the email.

## Never in a follow-up

- A price, discount, trial, timeline, feature or security claim the notes do not record.
- Another customer's name or result.
- A promise on behalf of someone else on your team who did not agree to it.
- Pressure phrases such as "last chance" or "before pricing changes".
