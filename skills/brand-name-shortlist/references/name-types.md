# Name types and tests

Read the table when generating (step 2) and the tests when screening survivors (step 5). Nothing here is a legal opinion.

## Name types: strengths and risks

| Type | Example shape | Strengths | Risks | How to screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | says what it does (two plain words) | explains itself; easy to search for the category | many lookalikes; hard to own; weak standing as a mark because it describes the goods | collision list matters most; expect the script to flag near matches |
| Invented | a made-up word | distinctive; often free in more places | no meaning to lean on; spelling and sound must carry it | run both tests hard; watch ambiguous spellings |
| Compound | two real words joined | readable at a glance; carries a feeling | many already taken; the join can read as another word | check the join, and the join with the suffix |
| Metaphor | an object or idea, unrelated to the category | memorable; room for a story | the association may be wrong in another language or culture; search results are crowded with the plain meaning | search the word plus the category before liking it |
| Acronym | initials | short; fits formal long names | says nothing; spoken as letters; shared with many organisations | only when the full name is already used in speech |

Spread the 30 to 60 candidates across these types; a list that is all one type usually means one idea repeated.

## Spelling and pronunciation tests

Run both on every survivor with people who have not seen the name.

1. **Hear-then-type.** Say the name aloud once, in a normal sentence. The listener types it. If fewer than 2 of 3 people type the exact spelling, the name will cost its owner in every phone call and podcast mention.
2. **Read-then-say.** Show the name written, no hint. The reader says it. If 2 of 3 differ from what you intend, rename or accept that you will explain it forever.
3. **Spoken-address test.** Say "go to <name> dot com" or "find us at <name>". Listen for a join that sounds like another word.

Common spelling traps the script flags: ph or f, c or k or s, ck, gh, ough, silent starts (kn, wr), ie or ei, doubled letters, and a trailing letter that merges with the dot ("tech" then ".com" gives "tech.com"; a name ending in c then ".com" gives a doubled c at the dot).

## Length and syllable guide

Aim for 2 to 3 syllables and 5 to 10 letters. Under 5 letters most short words and strings are taken or are hard to say; over 10 the name gets abbreviated by the people who use it, and the abbreviation becomes the real name. A one-syllable name works when it is a real, easy word.

## Items for the user to verify (never assert)

- Domain: the exact string for each suffix, at a registrar.
- Social handles on the platforms in use.
- App stores and package registries if the name will be an app or library.
- Trademark: search the registers of each country where the name will be used and the relevant classes. Starting points: https://tmsearch.uspto.gov (United States), https://www.tmdn.org/tmview (EU and several others), https://branddb.wipo.int (international). A clear search is not clearance; for a business, a trademark professional decides.
- Search results for the name alone and with the category word.
- Meaning and sound in each target language, by a native speaker.
- A company-registry name search where the business will be registered.
