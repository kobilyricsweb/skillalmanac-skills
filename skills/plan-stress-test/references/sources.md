# Sources behind the method

Facts used by this skill, restated in our own words, with where each comes from. Checked as ideas, not copied as text.

## Pre-mortem

Gary Klein's project pre-mortem asks a team to imagine that the project has already failed and to write down why, before work starts. The reason it helps: people who have been told the failure already happened find more and franker causes than people asked "what could go wrong". Source: Harvard Business Review, "Performing a Project Premortem", September 2007. URL: https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem

## Planning fallacy

Plans made from the inside view (this project's steps and intentions) run late and over budget more often than the outside view (how similar projects went) would predict. This is why pass 5 asks about first-time work, buffer placement and rehearsal. Source: Kahneman and Tversky, "Intuitive prediction: biases and corrective procedures", 1977 and 1979, summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_fallacy

## Critical path

The longest chain of dependent steps sets the earliest finish; slack on other chains does not help if that chain slips. This is why pass 3 asks for the longest chain of waits. Source: general project-scheduling method (critical path method); overview at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_path_method

## Risk as likelihood and impact

Ranking by likelihood times impact is a common convention in risk registers; its known weakness is false precision from small integer scales. The anchors in SKILL.md are there to keep the estimates comparable, not to make them exact. Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_matrix

## Single point of failure

A part whose loss stops the whole system. Applied here to people, accounts, vendors, approvals and slots in the calendar, not only to technical parts. Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_point_of_failure
