# Card and lesson patterns

## Turning source sentences into cards

| Source sentence shape | Card form | Tag type |
|---|---|---|
| Definition ("X is ...") | Front: the situation or description. Back: the term. Prefer this direction; a term-to-definition card is the weaker one. | `type:recall` |
| Cause ("X happens because Y") | Front: "Why does X happen?" Back: Y in one clause. | `type:explain` |
| Two things students confuse | Front names both and asks for the single deciding difference. | `type:contrast` |
| Process with ordered steps | One card per step transition ("What comes right after <step>?"), never one card with the full list. | `type:recall` |
| Rule or relationship | Front gives a new case with changed numbers or details. Back gives the prediction and the reason. | `type:apply` |
| Number or date | Front gives the thing, back gives the figure, copied from the source. Never round or add figures the source lacks. | `type:recall` |

Minimum mix for a deck of 15 or more cards: at least one fifth `type:apply` or `type:explain`. Tag every card with `topic:<short-name>`; add `src:<section>` when the source has sections, so a wrong card can be traced.

## Card rewrites (bad to good)

| Bad card | Problem the script reports | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Front: "What is chlorophyll, the green pigment?" Back: "Chlorophyll" | answer_leak | Front: "Which pigment absorbs light in a leaf?" Back: "Chlorophyll" |
| Front: "Explain the whole of respiration." Back: a paragraph | too_long | Three cards: where glycolysis happens, what the link reaction produces, which stage needs oxygen |
| Two cards: "What is osmosis?" and "what is OSMOSIS" | duplicate_front | Keep one; if the backs differ, one card is probably wrong or two facts are hiding in one |
| Back with 5 list items | too_long (lines) | One card per item, or a cloze-style prompt that asks for one missing item |

When a fix needs a fact the source does not give, do not supply it from memory: keep the card, mark it in the report, and ask.

## Lesson outline template (50 minutes)

Scale the minutes in proportion for other lengths; keep the order.

| Min | Segment | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| 0-6 | Opening retrieval | 3 short questions on earlier lessons (one from last lesson, one from 1 to 2 weeks ago, one from further back). Written from memory, no notes. |
| 6-8 | Goal | One sentence: what students will be able to do at the end, stated as a verb plus an object. |
| 8-20 | Chunk 1 | One idea only; a worked example; then check for understanding 1. |
| 20-32 | Chunk 2 | The next idea, including the likeliest misconception from the source; check for understanding 2. |
| 32-44 | Guided practice | Mixed problems or cases that need the student to choose which idea applies (interleaving); circulate and run check 3 on what you see. |
| 44-50 | Exit ticket | 2 or 3 items from this lesson, answered alone from memory, collected. |

Every outline also names: the misconception each check targets, the proceed threshold, and the fallback.

## Checks for understanding that need no quiz

| Technique | How it runs | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Mini-whiteboard or hands (all students answer at once) | Pose one question, count the correct answers, not volunteers | Fast whole-class read |
| Hinge question | One question whose wrong answers each reveal a different misconception | Deciding whether to move on |
| Cold call with a re-ask | Ask one student, then ask another to restate the reason | Reasoning, not recall |
| Predict then reveal | Students write a prediction before the example is shown | Setting up a misconception to correct |
| Explain to a neighbour in 30 seconds | Listen to three pairs | Spotting gaps in vocabulary |

Proceed threshold: about 80 percent of the class correct (S10 in `evidence.md`). Below it: re-teach with a different example, then re-check with a new question; do not repeat the same question. Between 80 and 100 percent: move on and note the students who missed it for the next opening retrieval.

## Spacing teacher items across lessons

Use the standard profile counted in lessons or school days: bring an exit-ticket item back in the opening retrieval after 1 day (or the next lesson), then after about 1 week, then about 2 weeks, then about 1 month. Put them on a calendar with the schedule command and the lesson date as the start date, or by hand if the class meets on fixed weekdays.
