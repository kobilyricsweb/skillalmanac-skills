## Reversibility classes

| Class | Meaning | Decision speed |
|---|---|---|
| Easy to undo | Can be reversed within weeks for little money or reputation | Decide fast with partial information; a delay costs more than a wrong turn |
| Costly to undo | Reversible, but money, months or relationships are lost | Decide on the matrix; set kill criteria and a review date |
| One-way | Cannot be undone, or undoing costs more than the benefit | Slow down; require a staged first step if one exists; the council's veto findings carry full weight |

When unsure between two classes, take the less reversible one.

## Writing reversibility lines

State four facts, not a feeling:
1. Class (one of the three above).
2. Cost to undo: money, time, and relationship or reputation, each with a figure or range.
3. Window: until when undoing is still possible.
4. Point of no return: the exact action or date after which it is not.

Also state the smaller first step that keeps the door open (a trial, a pilot, a month-to-month commitment instead of annual, a leave of absence instead of resignation). If none exists, write "none exists" and treat the choice as one-way.

## Kill criteria rules

A kill criterion is agreed before the start, so that quitting later is a pre-made decision and not an emotional one. Each needs:
- A signal that someone other than the decider can measure or observe.
- A threshold with a number or an unambiguous event.
- A check date, or a recurring cadence, that is on the calendar.
- An owner who looks, and who is not the person most invested.
- The action taken when it is crossed: stop, revert, or escalate for a re-decision. Name which.

Give at least two, covering different failure types (money, evidence about the key assumption, people, time). Derive them from the sceptic's early signals and the finance cash floor. Add one "review the whole decision" date even if no criterion is crossed.

## Examples (weak and strong)

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "If it is not going well, reconsider." | "If cash on hand is under 3 months of fixed costs on the 1st of any month, revert to employment and start applications that week; owner: partner." |
| "Watch customer feedback." | "If fewer than 6 of the first 20 pilot users are still active in week 6, stop the rollout and hold a re-decision meeting; owner: head of support." |
| "Give it a fair chance." | "Review on 1 June. Continue only if signed revenue is at least 15,000, else wind down by 30 June." |

## Staging a one-way choice

Look for the cheapest experiment that tests the load-bearing assumption: a paid pilot, a two-week trial, a reference call, a spend cap with a release condition, a lease with an exit clause. Recommend the stage first and the full commitment second, with the evidence that releases the second step written down.
