# Standing-desk blog: size of targetable search demand and first 5 articles

## Short answer

- **Targetable demand: about 60,000 searches a month in the US (Oct 2026 figures).** That is 24 distinct topics, not 36 keywords. It leaves out brand, off-topic and head-term searches; the working is below.
- Of that, about 34,000 a month sits in four of the five topics below (the comparison article has no volume in the export). That is the size of the pool, not a traffic forecast. Articles will capture only a small share of it.
- **Write first:** (1) best standing desk, (2) IKEA vs Uplift vs Flexispot comparison, (3) walking pad for a standing desk, (4) adjustable height desk, (5) standing desk converter.

## How I got from 318K to about 60K

| Step | Monthly searches (Oct 2026) |
|---|---|
| Sum of all 36 rows as exported | 318,240 |
| Merge singular/plural/reordered variants (they carry identical volume and are the same searches) | 167,140 (24 topics) |
| Remove competitor brand terms: uplift desk 22,200; ikea standing desk 14,800; flexispot standing desk 5,400 | 124,740 |
| Remove off-topic terms: "tiny desk standing room tickets" 1,300 (concert tickets) and "desk standing fan" 1,600 | 121,840 |
| Remove the head term "standing desk" (60,500) | **61,340** |

Why each cut:

- **Duplicates.** "standing desk" / "standing desks", "best standing desk" / "best standing desks" / "standing desk best" and the like have exactly the same volume. Google Ads reports overlapping variants. Adding them counts the same people two or three times. That alone inflates the total by almost half.
- **Brand terms.** People typing "uplift desk" want Uplift's site. A blog article will not win that search, and you would be sending them to a competitor's name. I count the 42,400 as demand you cannot target directly. The comparison angle is different, see topic 2.
- **Head term.** "standing desk" (60,500) is a broad, mostly shopping-intent search. Category pages and the big retailers own it. A blog post is unlikely to rank. It is a goal for the store's category page, not an article target.
- **Off-topic.** The ticket and fan terms have nothing to do with desks.

The 61K is a ceiling for the topics. It is not traffic. Typical click-through for an article ranking in the top 3 is a small fraction of the volume, and many of these terms will never reach the top 3 against established publishers.

## Data cautions

- **Seasonality and spikes.** "standing desk black friday" is 480 in every month except Nov 2025 (33,100) and Dec 2025 (1,900). Averaging it would give a false picture. I use Oct 2026 as the current level and treat Black Friday as a one-off seasonal spike.
- **Trends matter more than the Oct number for four terms.**
  - walking pad for standing desk: 590 to 5,400 (about 9x in 15 months, still rising every quarter)
  - standing desk for vr: 0 to 720 (new, small)
  - how to use a standing desk: 880 to 1,900 (doubling)
  - electric standing desk: 18,100 (Aug 2025) to 27,100 (Dec 2025) to 5,400 (Sep 2026), then 6,600 in Oct. That is down about 64% from Aug 2025. Do not size this topic from its early figures.
  - standing desk work from home: 8,100 to 1,900 (falling about 77%). Avoid.
- **Flat figures.** Most terms show exactly the same number for all 15 months. Google Ads rounds volumes into bands, so flat lines mean "no visible change", not "perfectly stable". Small differences between topics (for example 880 vs 1,000) are not meaningful.
- **Missing from the file.** There is no keyword difficulty, no cost per click, and no data on what ranks today. I could not rank by competition, and I can't confirm what intent a search has from this file alone. I judged intent from the wording.
- **Not in the export.** Queries such as "uplift vs flexispot" or "ikea bekant vs uplift" are not in the file. I don't know their volume. See topic 2.

## First 5 articles

| # | Topic | Oct 2026 searches/mo | Trend | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best standing desk (roundup, with a buyer's guide) | 9,900 | Flat | Biggest non-brand, non-head topic. Buyers comparing options, close to purchase. One page covers all three variants. |
| 2 | IKEA vs Uplift vs Flexispot, and how ours compares | Not in the export (brand terms total 42,400) | Flat | Your three named competitors draw 42,400 searches a month. A fair comparison is the one way to reach that audience with an article. Check volume for the "vs" queries before committing. |
| 3 | Walking pad for a standing desk (does it work, which to buy) | 5,400 | Up about 9x | Fastest-growing term. Fewer entrenched pages than "best standing desk", so it is the likeliest quick win. Pairs with a product you can sell. |
| 4 | Adjustable height desk (how to choose, electric vs manual) | 12,100 | Flat | Second-largest targetable topic. Same shoppers using different words. It also absorbs "electric standing desk" (6,600, volatile) so you don't need a separate page. |
| 5 | Standing desk converter vs full desk | 6,600 | Flat | Big and stable. People deciding between a cheap converter and a full desk are your buyers. A page that argues the case for a full desk fits a store that sells them. |

Next in line: how to use a standing desk (1,900, doubling), standing desk mat (4,400), standing desk chair (3,600), standing desk height calculator (1,900; a small tool page can earn links). Skip for now: benefits of a standing desk (2,900) is crowded with health sites, and cable tray, DIY plans and vs sitting are 1,000 or less each.

**Time-sensitive extra.** Black Friday 2026 is 27 Nov, a week away. Last year the term spiked to 33,100 in November. A short deals page, if you have an offer, could catch it, but only if it can be published and indexed this week. Otherwise plan one for next October.

## Assumptions

- "Targetable with articles" means informational or comparison searches that an independent blog post can win. I excluded brand navigation and head shopping terms. If you disagree, the 167K (de-duplicated) figure is the upper bound.
- Volumes are US monthly Google Ads figures as supplied. I did not check them against anything else.
- I did not know which products you sell (converters, walking pads, mats), so topics 3 and 5 assume you stock or could stock them.
