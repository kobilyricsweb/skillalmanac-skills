# Machine-prose patterns

Read the section that matches a finding from the scan, or the whole list when you cannot run the script. These are habits to question, not banned words.

## Vocabulary

Words that appear far more often in machine drafts than in human ones: delve, underscore, showcase, foster, leverage, harness, unlock, elevate, streamline, pivotal, multifaceted, seamless, robust, holistic, vibrant, intricate, myriad, transformative, cutting-edge, tapestry, realm, landscape, testament, beacon, cornerstone, synergy, paradigm, journey. Ask: would this author say it aloud? Swap for the plain word or delete the phrase.

## Inflation

Sentences that say a thing matters without saying why: "stands as a testament to", "plays a crucial role in", "marks a pivotal moment", "in today's fast-paced world". Replace with the concrete fact that earned the claim, if the text has one. If it has none, delete.

## Filler

Framing instead of content: "It is important to note that", "when it comes to", "in conclusion", "let's dive into", "in this article we will". Also wordy forms: "in order to" (to), "due to the fact that" (because), "has the ability to" (can). Delete or shorten.

## Residue

Text addressed to a chat partner, not a reader: "As an AI", "I hope this helps", "feel free to reach out", "great question". Delete. Keep a contact line only when the author gave one (an email address is a fact; keep it).

## Hedging

One hedge where the author is unsure is honest. Pairs and stacks ("could potentially possibly", "may arguably suggest") are reflex. Keep the single hedge that matches the evidence given in the text; drop the rest. Do not turn a hedge into a certainty.

## Parallelism

- Contrast template: "not just X, but Y", "it is not about X, it is Y", "more than just". State Y.
- Same opener: three or more sentences in a row beginning with the same word. Merge two, or lead one with its object or time.
- Trailing significance: ", highlighting the importance of", ", showcasing how". Cut, or move the real information into its own sentence.
- Triplets: lists of three adjectives or nouns used for rhythm. Keep only the ones with distinct meaning.

## Vagueness

"Experts say", "studies show", "research suggests" with no source in the text. Keep only if the text names the source; otherwise cut or flag for the author.

## Rhythm and punctuation

- Every sentence near the same length reads like a metronome. Merge or split a few, only where meaning allows.
- Frequent em dashes in place of commas, colons or full stops. Use the punctuation the sentence needs; leave a few where they carry a real aside.

## Rewrites that keep the facts

| Before | After | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| It is important to note that revenue grew 14% in Q3. | Revenue grew 14% in Q3. | Only framing removed; number kept |
| The tool could potentially help teams. | The tool may help teams. | One hedge kept; claim not strengthened |
| It is not just faster, but safer. | It is faster and safer. | Same two claims, no template |
| We tested it with 40 users, highlighting its reliability. | We tested it with 40 users. | The trailing phrase added no fact |
